This file states the author's intent and consent regarding the use of this
project's source code, documentation and history for machine learning. It is
a statement of the author's wishes and a notice to anyone who collects or
uses this material. The software itself is licensed under the GPL-3.0 (see
`LICENSE`).

## What is welcome

Training or improving machine learning models on this project is fine
**if the resulting model's weights are made publicly available** under terms
that allow anyone to download, use, inspect, modify and redistribute them.

The author does not require disclosure of training code, training data or
internal methods. The point is that knowledge learned from open code should
end up as an open resource. Those who built the model may keep their
know-how on how it was made.

## What is not authorized

The author does **not** consent to this project being used as training data
(including pre-training, fine-tuning, distillation or retrieval-augmented
corpora) for any model whose weights are not publicly available, including
models that are only reachable via a paid or closed API, a subscription, or
an internal service.

## Scope

- Applies to the source code, documentation, commit history, issues and any
  other material in this repository, and to copies or derivatives of them.
- Applies to anyone who gathers this material, whether by scraping, mirroring
  or bulk download, and regardless of whether the collector is the one who
  later trains a model.
- "Publicly available weights" means that anyone can obtain the weights
  without payment, and may use, modify and redistribute them.

## Notice to crawlers and dataset builders

If you operate a crawler or build datasets: this is the author's explicit
statement that scraping this repository for closed-weight model training is
not permitted. If you are building an open-weights model and want to use
this material, you are welcome to, with no need to ask.

If you are unsure whether your use qualifies, or want to discuss an
exception, please open an issue.

## Why

Training large models on open-source work at scale puts significant cost on
the infrastructure that hosts it, concentrates the resulting value in a few
hands, and has contributed to maintainer burnout. Open weights return some of
that value to the commons that produced it.
